# Content plan

What to teach after unit 14, and in what order. The aim is for learners to read complete texts
— real stelae, whole Book of the Dead spells, all of the Shipwrecked Sailor, Ptahhotep's
epilogue — the way they read the Library's excerpts today: every line taught, spoken and
practised. The numbers were measured on 29 October 2026 against the AES corpus with
`tools/aes.py` and the course as it stood (14 units); they will drift as the course grows.

This plan is the content backlog. It takes in the six units proposed in `tools/aes.py` (`UNITS`)
and places each where a text needs it.

## Rules for new content

1. **Every line is taught.** As now: each line of a text is a phrase with its exercises and its
   clip, and every word and sign in it is taught before it.
2. **Finished lessons are never rewritten.** New lines go into new lessons, so a learner's
   finished lessons stay finished. A short text grows in place: its new lines are taught in a
   later unit, which ends by reading the whole text again. A long work becomes a series of
   chapters, each its own reading text, grouped under the work in the Library.
3. **One grammar point per unit, met in a text.** A unit teaches the words and the one
   construction its reading needs, about 15–25 words like units 10–14, and ends with the reading.
4. **The next text is the one that needs the fewest new words** at that point. Measure before
   writing.
5. **Real spellings.** Middle Kingdom texts keep AES's hieroglyphs, with scribal variants mapped
   to signs the course teaches (V31A to V31, Z7 to the w coil, Z2A and Z3 to Z2). AES has no
   hieroglyphs for the Book of the Dead: there each word takes its commonest attested spelling,
   and the text's source line says so.
6. **Our own English.** Translations are written from the Egyptian, checked against the TLA's
   German (and its English, where it has one), and credited as below.

## Where the course stands

2 sections, 14 units, 75 lessons: 158 words, about 130 signs and 104 phrases. 8 Library texts
with 25 lines. The course's words cover 61.6% of Middle Kingdom literature.

Grammar taught so far: suffix and dependent pronouns; nominal, adjectival and adverbial sentences;
sḏm=f and sḏm.n=f; participles; the future with r and the infinitive; negation with n and nn;
orders and m "don't"; the genitive. Not yet: the stative, relative forms, ḥr with the infinitive,
the passive, sḏm.jn=f, wishes (sḏm=f "may he…"), jr "if", tm "not to". In Middle Kingdom
literature the commonest of these are relative forms (13.3 per 1,000 words), the stative (12.1)
and the passive (3.7).

## The sequence

Each step is measured after everything before it, so its new words are what it really costs.
Names are counted apart: they are sounded out, not learned. AES text ids are in brackets.

### What it adds up to

About 550 new words and 45 names, about 400 lines (430 with Westcar), and about 170 sign codes
before variants are merged. Afterwards the course's words cover 77.7% of Middle Kingdom literature (61.6%
today): the Shipwrecked Sailor 100%, Ptahhotep 86%, Westcar 80%, Sinuhe 78%, the Peasant 78%.

Today 75 lessons teach about 390 items. The plan adds about 1,100 (words, signs, lines), so
roughly 200 lessons or 35–40 units. It ships in steps: 0.4 with Phase 0 and Section 3, 0.5 with
Section 4, 0.6 with the Sailor, 0.7 with the Teachings.

## Work the plan needs besides content

1. **Texts taught in more than one lesson.** A text that grows in place is read again at the end
   of the lesson that completes it. Check that the reading finale, "Read" in the Library and the
   course tests all accept a second reading lesson for the same text.
2. **Works and chapters in the Library.** Group a work's chapters under one heading in story
   order, with its total readability, and an amulet for reading a whole work.
3. **Review load.** About 400 more lines in spaced review will lengthen daily reviews. Measure it
   with the careless-learner walk before Section 5. If it is too much, review a chapter's lines
   together, or less often than words and signs.
4. **An authoring tool.** A `tools/texts.py` that turns an AES passage into draft phrases (tokens
   with lemma ids matched to course words, normalised spellings, the German gloss) and lists the
   new words and signs. It should also pick the attested spellings for the Book of the Dead.
